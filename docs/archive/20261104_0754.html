D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward Models
Authors: Marius Memmel, Shawn Yin, Urvi Rutia, Angad Wadhwa, Eldon Li, Gavin Angeloff, Ankit Goyal, Jesse Zhang, Dieter Fox, Fabio Ramos, Anqi Li, Abhishek Gupta
First: 2026-11-02T18:52:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:52:11+00:00
Comments: Website at https://weirdlabuw.github.io/dna
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold promise for zero-shot video task-progress estimation in robot learning, yet existing frameworks frequently suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions due to implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking designs. Naive prompting of frontier models collapses into a binary success detector that fails to capture nuanced, non-linear progress and failures. While post-training approaches can mitigate this through explicit training, they demand expensive real-world robotics data and require computationally intensive post-training for every individual base model. To resolve these limitations, we introduce D&A (Describe & Assess): our approach decouples progress estimation into an objective, task-grounded textual description of scene dynamics followed by an assessment phase using explicit rubrics. By compelling the VLM to synthesize an explicit, text-based definition of absolute success before scoring, the framework anchors evaluations to an external, global criterion rather than the video's internal timeline. Empirical results demonstrate that our method calibrates zero-shot estimates and mitigates confirmation bias, providing fine-grained, partial progress metrics that accurately penalize failed executions while correctly interpolating successful runs. Being purely prompt-based, D&A generalizes across tasks, embodiments, and benchmarks---matching or surpassing post-trained reward models---and its reward estimates enable efficient downstream reinforcement learning.

HierFlow: Hierarchical Coupled Dual-Space Search for Automatic Agentic Workflow Generation
Authors: Dong Li, Yanchi Liu, Xujiang Zhao, Wei Cheng, Zhengzhang Chen, Xintao Wu, Zhong Chen, Haifeng Chen, Chen Zhao
First: 2026-05-20T01:56:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:51:02+00:00

Abstract
Although structured workflows empower Large Language Models (LLMs) to tackle complex problems, automating their creation is severely hindered by a vast combinatorial search space, frequently resulting in inflexible and resource-heavy offline training dependencies. To address this, we conceptualize workflow generation as an intertwined topology-and-execution search paradigm, where the broader topological layer dictates subtask boundaries and lower-level execution outcomes actively reshape the topology itself. Building on this foundation, we introduce HierFlow, a training-free, test-time hierarchical search architecture that automates agentic workflow design by merging feedback-guided topology adjustments with a fast, MCTS-inspired tree search for sub-workflow optimization. HierFlow maximizes efficiency through an intelligent gating module that selectively triggers execution-level searches based on contextual necessity, a mechanism we further support with an in-depth analysis detailing how varying degrees of cross-task coupling impact the effectiveness of hierarchical splitting. Comprehensive testing across question answering, mathematical reasoning, and code generation benchmarks confirms that HierFlow consistently outperforms strong baselines, delivering an optimal balance of high-quality results and computational efficiency without any additional training overhead.

CoSpaDi: Compressing LLMs via Calibration-Guided Sparse Dictionary Learning

Abstract
Post-training LLM compression often relies on low-rank approximations, which force all columns of a projection matrix to share a single low-dimensional subspace. We propose CoSpaDi, a training-free compression framework that replaces this single-subspace assumption with a union-of-subspaces model via sparse dictionary learning. CoSpaDi factorizes each weight matrix into a dense dictionary and column-sparse coefficients, allowing different columns to select different subsets of dictionary atoms at the same storage budget. To preserve model behavior, we use calibration activations to transform functional reconstruction into a standard dictionary learning problem. Across LLaMA and Qwen models, CoSpaDi improves accuracy-compression and perplexity-compression trade-offs over SVD-based and structured pruning baselines at 20-40\% compression ratios, while naturally supporting sparse-dense computation and post-training quantization of sparse coefficients.

PlantVLM-Edge: Quantized Vision-Language Models for On-Device Crop Health Diagnosis
Authors: Gaurab Wagle, Bishal Adhikari, Jiajia Li, Xiaolei Huang, Dong Chen
First: 2026-11-02T18:19:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:19:19+00:00

Abstract
Timely and accurate crop health diagnosis is essential for sustainable agricultural production, yet accurate diagnosis often requires expert knowledge that is not readily available. Recent vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in visual understanding, reasoning, and natural-language interaction, creating new opportunities for intelligent crop health assessment. However, deploying VLMs on resource-constrained devices remains challenging due to their substantial computational and memory requirements. This paper presents PlantVLM-Edge, a quantized vision-language framework for conversational crop health diagnosis that can run entirely on resource-constrained edge devices. PlantVLM-Edge combines agricultural domain adaptation, post-training quantization, and on-device serving with an offline chat interface, allowing growers to ask open-ended questions and receive diagnoses, symptom explanations, and management guidance without cloud connectivity. The framework is backbone-agnostic: we validate it with two different VLM types with varying scale and architecture (Qwen3-VL-2B and LLaVA-1.5-7B), four quantization schemes, and two diagnostic domains (plant diseases and insect pests), and we deploy all variants on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin. Eight-bit quantization affects answer quality minimally relative to the full-precision model, and 4-bit activation-aware weight quantization (AWQ) retains 98.6% of full-precision answer quality on both domains while reducing the weight memory of the 2B backbone by 49%. In its most compact form, PlantVLM-Edge requires 2.01 GiB of weight memory, returns its first token in about 0.12 s, and completes a 64-token diagnostic response in 0.78 s. These results demonstrate that timely and accurate crop health diagnosis is practical on edge devices, providing a foundation for next-generation crop monitoring and decision support systems.

Same Outputs, Different Scores: Auditing the Unchecked Graders of Multimodal LLM Benchmarks
Authors: Ahmad Jarjis Hasan, Jonathan Lwowski
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-11-02T16:20:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:20:07+00:00
Comments: Accepted at WACV 2027 (Evaluations and Datasets Track). Code and data: https://github.com/ahmadhasan2k8/grader-audit
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Every multimodal-LLM multiple-choice benchmark ends with a scoring rule that converts a model's response, often free-form text rather than a clean option letter, into a right/wrong verdict, buried in its code. Unlike the dataset, this rule is chosen without justification and left unexamined, yet it sets the published number. We reproduce the official scorers of eight peer-reviewed benchmarks on five open vision-language models (VLMs) that fit a single 16 GB GPU, and introduce a blinded human-plus-LLM-judge adjudication (Cohen's kappa=0.956) that recovers what the model actually answered. The scoring rule alone moves accuracy by up to 22 points on identical outputs, yet exactly 0 wherever a model emits clean option letters, so it hides on strong-model leaderboards. It is the fallback and not the rambling that does the damage: the benchmark with the highest non-compliance we measure has an artifact of exactly zero, because its scorer abstains instead of guessing; a reasoning model with non-compliance 0.000 still swings 9.5 points with which regex match of its trace the parser keeps. No standard rule is reliable: snapping a non-answer to its closest option credits answers never given yet buries correct ones, abstention discards answers stated in words, and likelihood scoring follows answer-token priors, so a single grade conflates capability with luck rather than measuring either. We release all code, labels, and the protocol, and argue benchmarks should report a non-compliance rate and at least two rules, not one unexamined grade.

Authors: Mohamed Atef Abouelhadid, Diaa Addeen Abuhani, Muhammad Akhtar Munir, Fahad Shahbaz Khan, Salman Khan

Abstract
Earth observation (EO) workflows require selecting among known outcomes, estimating confidence, and processing large volumes of imagery. Generative vision-language models can address these tasks, but autoregressive decoding is computationally costly and their confidence estimates are unreliable. Decision models offer an alternative, returning probabilities over candidate answers in a single forward pass. However, their accuracy, calibration, and transfer across EO tasks remain insufficiently understood. We investigate these properties through a diagnostic study of d3-flash (8.4B parameters) across 30 settings from 23 public EO benchmarks. We find that compositional reasoning transfers well and calibration improves over its Qwen3.5-9B backbone on 24 settings, while counting, change quantification, damage grading, and absent-object recognition remain challenging. To address these limitations, we adapt d3-flash through low-rank fine-tuning on public EO training splits, producing EO-Jev. EO-Jev improves significantly over d3-flash on 26 of 30 settings and matches or exceeds specialist references on 20 of 28. It also achieves an average gain of 8.0 points across 12 held-out settings and lower expected calibration error than specialists on 21 of 28 settings. These improvements come at a modest cost of 1.8 points in general decision performance. Our results demonstrate the potential of adapting general decision models into competitive EO generalists while highlighting persistent challenges in quantitative perception, option-order consistency, and calibration under distribution shift.

Abstract
**Purpose:** Ultrasound biometry is widely used across various clinical applications, including prenatal and cardiac examinations, to obtain quantitative measurements by placing calipers at anatomical landmarks. However, these measurements remain subject to interoperator variability as they rely largely on manual caliper placement. Conventional methods often use specialized prediction heads for different ultrasound tasks, whereas vision-language models offer flexibility through text instructions. However, direct coordinate prediction does not explicitly model spatial likelihood distributions, limiting the representation of localization ambiguity. **Methods:** We propose Kev-Bio, a vision-language model that uses clinical measurement questions to guide ultrasound landmark detection. Inspired by System One decision models, Kev-Bio uses decision representations of the language model to generate queries for each caliper endpoint. These queries are matched against shared dense ultrasound features to produce spatial probability maps, from which landmark coordinates and clinical measurements are derived. The model is trained end-to-end using both landmark localization and clinical measurement losses. **Results:** On the nine-view FU/GU Biometry 2026 dataset, Kev-Bio achieves a mean radial error (MRE) of 22.50 pixels and a clinical measurement mean absolute error (MAE) of 15.38, outperforming the compared baselines in overall performance. **Conclusion:** These results demonstrate the effectiveness of Kev-Bio for ultrasound landmark detection and clinical measurement across different anatomical views. Our code and models are available at https://github.com/moeinheidari7829/Kev-Bio.

Matching in Context: Temporal Fusion and Query Adaptation for Training-Free Video Temporal Grounding
Authors: Zhuo Cao, Bingqing Zhang, Sen Wang, Xue Li
First: 2026-09-08T15:03:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:00:40+00:00
Comments: 10 pages, 4 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Text-guided Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) localizes moments in an untrimmed video that correspond to a text query. Supervised approaches require costly temporal annotations and task-specific training, yet can generalize poorly under distribution shift. To reduce this dependence on task-specific supervision, training-free approaches use pretrained vision-language models to localize moments by measuring the similarity between video and query features. However, independently encoded frames provide limited temporal context, while a fixed query embedding can assign similar scores to different candidate moments within the video. To address these limitations, we propose DSE-VTG, a \underline{D}ual-\underline{S}ide \underline{E}nhancement framework that combines visual evidence across temporal granularities with video-specific query adaptation, without task-specific training. On the visual side, Multi-scale Similarity Fusion (MSF) combines frame- and clip-level similarities into a unified, temporally aware similarity profile. On the query side, Query-level Test-Time Adaptation (Q-TTA) uses pseudo-labels to optimize a lightweight additive offset for more discriminative matching within the video, without fine-tuning the backbone or calling external large language models. Experiments on three standard and two out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks show gains over the prior training-free methods. On Charades-STA, DSE-VTG improves mIoU by 5.89 percentage points over the strongest compared baseline under the same backbone. Under distribution shift, DSE-VTG reaches 50.86 mIoU on Charades-CG Novel-Word, surpassing the strongest supervised baseline by 2.76 mIoU. Our code will be released upon acceptance.

Abstract
Geospatial text spotting involves locating and transcribing geographic labels, such as road names, district names, and landmarks, in raster maps. These labels are often small, densely arranged, and arbitrarily oriented. Although Arabic text recognition has been studied extensively in documents and natural scenes, Arabic geospatial text spotting remains underexplored. We introduce ARABGEOTEXT, an Arabic-centric benchmark covering 970 geographic locations in Riyadh and Jeddah, with 17,875 manually annotated text instances per view type. For each location, we pair an OpenStreetMap (OSM) rendering with a composite created by overlaying that rendering on the corresponding remote sensing (RS) imagery. The paired views preserve label content and pixel geometry, allowing evaluation across different backgrounds. Geographic partitions support in-city and city-disjoint evaluation of detection, recognition, and end-to-end spotting. Experiments with direct Qwen3.5-4B spotting show difficulties in recovering all labels within a tile. We therefore propose a tool-augmented vision-language framework that combines YOLO26 localization and crop rectification with Qwen3.5-4B recognition adapted through low-rank adaptation (LoRA). The framework outperforms direct LoRA-adapted Qwen3.5-4B spotting across both image views and test cities. With localization held fixed, reader adaptation further improves recognition over the pretrained zero-shot reader. The dataset and code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/yakoubbazi/ArabGeoText.

GibbsLens: Training-Free Camera Control via Block-Conditional Gibbs Refinement
Authors: Yuzhu Wang, Xi Ye, Duo Su, Yangyang Xu, Jun Zhu
First: 2026-05-12T09:51:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T15:31:32+00:00
Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS2026

Abstract
Training-free camera control for pretrained flow-matching video generators is a partial-observation inverse problem: a depth-warped guidance video supplies noisy evidence on a subset of latent sites, which the sampler must reconcile with the pretrained prior. Existing methods struggle to balance the trade-off between trajectory adherence and visual quality and the heuristic guidance-strength tuning lacks robustness. We propose GibbsLens, which resolves this dilemma through a structural change to the sampler: each outer hard-replacement guidance step is augmented with an inner-loop block-conditional pseudo-Gibbs refinement on the unobserved complement at the same noise level, with provable convergence to the partial-observation conditional data law. To accelerate convergence on high-dimensional video latents, we exploit their conditional locality, partitioning the unobserved complement into 3D patches, each tracked by a custom mixing indicator that adaptively freezes converged patches. On RealEstate10K and DAVIS, GibbsLens attains the best FVD against all seven training-free and training-based competitors, outperforming every training-free baseline on every reported metric. R$^2$-Omni: Reducing Structural and Precision Redundancy in Omni-LLMs for Long-Form Generation
Authors: Bolong Feng, Pengfei Zhou, Zhenglin Wan, Wei Wang, Wangbo Zhao
First: 2026-11-02T14:57:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T14:57:48+00:00
Comments: 32 pages, 9 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Omni-modal large language models (Omni-LLMs) typically process large numbers of multimodal tokens from audio-visual inputs, incurring substantial computational costs and a large memory footprint for the key-value (KV) cache. Aggressive token pruning can alleviate this problem while maintaining competitive performance on multiple-choice tasks, which require only a single option as output. However, we observe substantial quality degradation when generating long-form, open-ended responses. We attribute this gap to query-guided selection, which can retain sufficient evidence for answer selection while discarding details needed for comprehensive generation. This motivates reducing overlapping information across tokens, termed structural redundancy, while preserving diverse content. However, the retained representations may still contain precision redundancy due to unnecessarily high numerical precision in their cached values. To address these two levels of redundancy in token representations, we introduce a training-free framework named R$^2$-Omni. First, Structure-Aware Token Consolidation (STC) consolidates redundant representations using spatiotemporal similarity in video and local continuity in audio. Then, Semantic-Sensitivity Guided Precision Allocation (SSPA) allocates value precision to the retained representations according to the estimated reduction in attention-output error under quantized keys, with query attention and audio-visual alignment providing a semantic prior. Extensive experiments on long-form generation benchmarks demonstrate an average 5.0$\times$ reduction in the memory footprint while preserving 97.98% of baseline generation quality and achieving a 1.48$\times$ end-to-end speedup, highlighting the benefits of jointly reducing structural and precision redundancy.

Abstract
Optical Character Recognition (OCR) is essential for digitizing historical archives, yet standard evaluation metrics such as Character Error Rate (CER) do not necessarily reflect whether meaningful information, such as names, dates, and identifiers, is preserved. In this work, we investigate entitylevel analysis of OCR errors as a complementary perspective for evaluating OCR quality in historical documents. Using a publicly available benchmark of 175 documents from the Berrutti Archive, augmented with 5,209 manually annotated entity mentions that will be released upon acceptance, we compare sequence-recognition OCR systems, OCR-specialized vision-language models, and general-purpose vision-language models. Beyond global transcription accuracy, we analyze entity preservation and recoverability under varying character-level edit tolerances. Our experiments show that OCR rankings depend on the evaluation criterion: while Qwen 3.6 achieves the highest entity coverage across all tested tolerances, several systems with lower exact-match coverage recover substantially when small errors are tolerated, suggesting that many of their errors correspond to localized character-level deviations rather than fundamentally incorrect entity transcriptions. Conversely, some systems remain unable to recover many entities even under relaxed matching. These findings show that OCR quality cannot be fully characterized by aggregate transcription metrics alone and highlight the importance of evaluating how OCR errors affect the preservation and recoverability of meaningful information.

Abstract
An image that is hard to classify on its own may have a helpful neighbor in the same unlabeled batch. Zero-shot vision-language models (VLMs) score images independently and leave this evidence unused. We study small unlabeled query batches with no labeled examples. Visual similarity can reflect pose or background, so it may connect images from different classes. Each image's scores over the class vocabulary offer another way to choose neighbors. We introduce Behavioral Affinity Transduction (BAT), which builds a sparse graph from these class-response profiles. The graph supplies neighbor evidence during prediction. Across diverse datasets, BAT improves the mean accuracy of every tested readout. In the main RN50 comparison, soft and hard EM-Dirichlet gain $4.22$ and $2.72$ percentage points.

Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have shown strong potential in robotic surgery for question answering, scene understanding, and grounded assistance, but their high computational cost limits real clinical deployment. Knowledge distillation provides a practical way to build compact surgical MLLMs, yet direct distillation is not well suited to surgical data because surgical samples vary greatly in difficulty, visual ambiguity, and required clinical context, making uniform teacher-student transfer unstable. In addition, the role of supervision changes during training: early stages require stronger teacher guidance for multimodal alignment, while later stages rely more on task supervision for effective optimization. To address these challenges, we propose SurgPace, a complexity-aware continuous curriculum framework for progressive distillation of surgical vision-language models. SurgPace estimates sample complexity using predictive confidence and Lorentz geometric consistency, and uses the fused score to guide a continuous easy-to-hard training process. We further introduce hierarchical representation alignment, which aligns the student with the teacher at the feature, logit, and probability levels, while gradually shifting the training focus from teacher imitation to task supervision. This design separates sample-level curriculum from objective-level scheduling, leading to more stable and effective distillation. We evaluate SurgPace on Surg-396K, a benchmark built from four public surgical datasets and covering five dialogue paradigms. SurgPace consistently outperforms strong MLLM distillation baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness for building compact, memory-efficient surgical MLLMs with faster decoding under controlled benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/hezhicheng2002/SurgPace.

Abstract
Visual encoders construct a representation of the image input for Vision-Language models. How much conceptual, as opposed to immediately visible, information does this representation contain? M-PFT: Angular Margin for Calibrated Prompt-Tuning of Fine-Tuned Vision-Language Models
Authors: Shihab Aaqil Ahamed, Mohamed Ansaff, Nang Khin Myat Lay Phyu, Udaya Sampath K. Perera Miriya Thanthrige, Chamira U. S. Edussooriya, Ranga Rodrigo, Muhammad Haris Khan

Abstract
Prompt-tuning has emerged as a parameter-efficient technique for adapting large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) to downstream tasks while keeping the pre-trained weights frozen. However, prompt-tuned VLMs often suffer from a base-to-novel dual miscalibration problem, where models typically show underconfidence on base classes and overconfidence on novel classes. Current VLM calibration approaches address this dual miscalibration either separately or jointly, but lack a geometric understanding of the hyperspherical embedding space and thus fail to exploit angular discriminative properties to adjust distorted decision boundaries. To address this, we propose M-PFT, a novel angular-margin approach for prompt tuning that introduces two complementary objectives to promote class discriminability and semantic anchoring. This enlarges the angular margin by 1) minimizing the maximum cosine similarity between tunable class embeddings to reduce embedding collapse and 2) minimizing the angular distance between each tunable embedding and its corresponding zero-shot embedding to preserve semantic geometry. Extensive experiments across 9 prompt-tuning methods with various backbones on 15 diverse datasets show that our approach reduces the average calibration error on novel classes, while remaining competitive on base classes and maintaining comparable accuracy. Further analyses reveal that M-PFT effectively adjusts distorted decision boundaries induced by prompt tuning while preserving the pre-trained embedding space, improving calibration with negligible computational overhead.

DR Pixel: Page-Native Deep Research with Vision-Language Models
Authors: Tiansheng Hu, Yichuan Wang, James V. Talwar, Nicholas Tomlin, Chen Zhao, Sewon Min
First: 2026-11-02T13:13:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T13:13:42+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Complex documents such as scientific papers contain rich information in tables, figures, and equations; however, existing deep research (DR) agents typically reduce papers to OCR-extracted text. We instead ask whether vision-language models (VLMs) can enable page-native deep research: directly retrieving and reasoning over rendered document page images. We introduce DR Pixel, the first open-source end-to-end multimodal DR agent that uses rendered page images as its sole retrieval and reasoning unit, operating over a corpus of 1.06M pages. To evaluate this setting, we construct DRPixelBench, a benchmark on scientific papers requiring reasoning over text, figures, tables, and equations. Surprisingly, page-native DR already works well without training, outperforming OCR-based baselines across state-of-the-art models like GPT-5.4, Gemini-3.5-Flash, and Qwen3.6-27B, while requiring fewer tool calls. We next turn to DR training. A key challenge is that teacher trajectories for agentic supervised finetuning are noisy, mixing critical reasoning and action steps with redundant or failed attempts. To address this, we introduce counterfactual credit reweighting, a fine-grained credit assignment method that weights individual reasoning and action steps by their counterfactual contribution to the final answer. Our training improves retrieval recall by about 15 points and final accuracy by over 10 points on DRPixelBench, and likewise outperforms the untrained baseline on four existing benchmarks. On out-of-distribution ViDoRe-V3, DR Pixel comes within 1.8 points of an oracle that is given the gold pages directly. Our results suggest that the conventional OCR-then-retrieve pipeline is not only unnecessary but suboptimal: directly retrieving and reasoning over visual document pages makes complex DR both more effective and more efficient.

Abstract
Image-text corpora contain copies of one image, captions that describe one image in different ways, captions reused for different images, and captions that do not match their images. Each case calls for a different curation action. Deduplication and multimodal entity resolution (ER) only decide whether records are the same, and they are evaluated on labeled pairs. Abstract

Abstract
Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is shifting from rigid, step-by-step instruction following toward open-vocabulary, goal-oriented autonomy. Abstract
Interleaved multimodal chain-of-thought reasoning improves the visual reasoning capabilities of multimodal large language models by dynamically incorporating visual evidence during reasoning. However, existing methods primarily select evidence from historical context. When the reasoning focus shifts, history-guided evidence may remain focused on prior regions and fail to cover the visual information required by the next step. If the coverage is sufficient, the history-guided evidence is retained; otherwise, LPAVR switches to the lookahead-guided evidence. This better aligns the visual context with upcoming reasoning and improves consistency between draft predictions and target-model decoding. Experiments on multiple multimodal reasoning benchmarks show that LPAVR improves both reasoning accuracy and the acceptance rates of MTP speculative tokens, jointly enhancing visual reasoning and speculative decoding efficiency.
