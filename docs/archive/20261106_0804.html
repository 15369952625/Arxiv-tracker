Quantum-Inspired Tensor Network Autoencoders for Anomaly Detection: A MERA-Based Approach
Authors: Emre Gurkanli, Michael Spannowsky
First: 2026-04-08T00:33:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T18:19:10+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 5 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We investigate whether a multiscale tensor-network architecture provides a useful inductive bias for reconstruction-based anomaly detection in collider jets. Jets are produced by a branching cascade, so their internal structure is naturally organised across angular and momentum scales. This motivates an autoencoder that compresses information hierarchically and can reorganise short-range correlations before coarse-graining. Guided by this picture, we formulate a Multiscale Entanglement Renormalization Ansatz (MERA)-inspired autoencoder acting on ordered jet constituents. To our knowledge, a MERA-inspired autoencoder has not previously been proposed, and this architecture has not been explored in collider anomaly detection. We compare this architecture to a dense autoencoder, the corresponding tree-tensor-network limit, and classical baselines within a background-only reconstruction framework. The paper is organised around two main questions: whether locality-aware hierarchical compression is genuinely supported by the data, and whether the disentangling layers of MERA contribute beyond a simpler tree hierarchy. To address these questions, we combine benchmark comparisons with a training-free local-compressibility diagnostic and a direct identity-disentangler ablation. In the nominal benchmark, the MERA-inspired model attains a higher mean AUC than the dense autoencoder at all three latent dimensions while using roughly one-third of its trainable parameters. The locality-preserving multiscale structure is well matched to jet data, and, at the strongest compression bottleneck, the trained disentanglers move substantially away from the identity and are accompanied by a positive mean performance shift. Overall, the study supports locality-aware hierarchical compression as a useful and parameter-efficient inductive bias for reconstruction-based jet anomaly detection in this benchmark setting.

Visual Token Routing: Learning to keep tokens that matter
Authors: Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck
First: 2026-11-04T18:10:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T18:10:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Computer-use agents (CUAs) have recently achieved superhuman performance on several long-horizon benchmarks. However, their computational cost limits their use in long-horizon tasks and real-time domains such as video games. A key bottleneck is representing each screenshot as thousands of visual tokens, which accumulate into long contexts as agents reason over extended interaction histories. We observe that most of these visual tokens are either semantically irrelevant to the agent's next action or temporally redundant across screenshots. Building on this observation, we introduce Visual Token Routing (VTR), a joint training framework for learning sparse visual representations that preserve the information needed for action prediction. In VTR, we train the vision-language model together with a lightweight spatio-temporal router that examines each image region in the current and previous screenshots and decides whether its visual token enters the language model. We optimize the router using an adaptive loss to keep the number of visual tokens as small as possible while minimizing performance degradation. With just 2,000 training steps, VTR matches the performance of the full-resolution model on OSWorld, CUA-World, and video game action prediction, while using 2.5-5.8x fewer visual tokens. These savings reduce inference cost by up to 3.3x and speed up prefill by up to 2.9x. VTR also outperforms lowering the image resolution and other baselines for dropping tokens, by up to 12.8 points on OSWorld. Overall, VTR enables computer-use agents to process only the visual tokens they need, allowing them to be used in long-horizon tasks and real-time domains at a fraction of the computational cost. ESS-Flow: Training-free guidance as Bayesian inference in source space
Authors: Adhithyan Kalaivanan, Zheng Zhao, Jens Sjölund, Fredrik Lindsten

Abstract
Guiding pretrained flow-based generative models for conditional generation or to produce samples with desired target properties enables solving diverse tasks without retraining on paired data. We present ESS-Flow, a gradient-free method that leverages the Gaussian prior of the source distribution in flow-based models to perform Bayesian inference directly in the source space using elliptical slice sampling. ESS-Flow only requires forward passes through the generative model and observation process, making it applicable even when gradients are unavailable, such as with non-differentiable simulation-based observations or quantization in the generation process. We demonstrate its effectiveness on scientific applications including generating materials with target properties, protein backbone structures with improved designability, and synthesizable, drug-like molecules.

Accelerating Rectified Flow Models via Trajectory-Aware Caching
Authors: Xiao Liu, Kai Liu, Naiyang Guan, Hongliang Lu, Zhixin Wang, Zhikai Chen, Renjing Pei, Yulun Zhang
First: 2026-05-16T03:44:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T17:29:39+00:00
Comments: 22 pages,14 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Diffusion and rectified flow (RF) models generate high-fidelity images and videos, but their iterative velocity-field evaluations are computationally expensive. Existing caching methods accelerate sampling by skipping timesteps, yet their coarse approximations introduce accumulated errors over long skip intervals and degrade quality under aggressive acceleration. We propose TACache (Trajectory-Aware Cache), a training-free acceleration framework following a skip-then-compensate paradigm. TACache performs an orthogonal decomposition of discrete velocity acceleration along the RF trajectory into a parallel component and an orthogonal residual, isolating the magnitude and directional sources of per-step approximation error. The framework operates in two stages: offline, cumulative variation thresholds on the magnitude and direction indicators yield the skip schedule and bound how far each skip interval may extend; online, at each skipped step the offline statistics are combined with the sample's historical orthogonal direction to reconstruct the skipped velocity without additional model evaluations. Main comparisons on BAGEL, FLUX.1-dev, and Wan2.1-1.3B show that TACache achieves up to $4.11\times$ speedup on text-to-image generation and $2.11\times$ speedup on text-to-video generation, with consistent improvements over prior cache-based methods on most quality metrics. Our code is available at https://github.com/XiaoLiu1237/TACache.

AdaLoop: Thinking with the Vision Encoder
Authors: Xinran Nicole Han, Yiyang Lu, Katelyn Gan, Todd Zickler
First: 2026-10-15T06:14:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T17:03:54+00:00
Comments: Technical report. Code at https://github.com/xrhan/AdaLoop
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Most vision-language models (VLMs) treat the vision encoder as a static feature extractor: it encodes an image once, independently of the question, and leaves subsequent reasoning to the language model. Yet different questions may require different visual operations and processing depths. We present AdaLoop, a framework for question-conditioned visual recurrence in VLMs. AdaLoop adapts visual computation to the question and reuses the encoder's transformations across multiple iterations, with the pretrained vision weights frozen. We also introduce VisRO, a procedural dataset and generation pipeline for training and evaluating vision-centric reasoning. On six VisRO tasks, AdaLoop outperforms standard fine-tuning and question-conditioned single-pass baselines with matched training data and optimizer updates. These gains hold across three VLM families on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution examples. Our findings suggest that pretrained vision encoders can contribute to reasoning through task-directed, iterative visual computation.

Abstract

EvoWorld: Towards Out-of-Sight World Evolution in Generative Video Models
Authors: Yequan Bie, Dengyang Jiang, Zhixuan Chen, Xiaozhuan Liang, Haoxuan Che, Quande Liu, Xi Chen, Hao Chen
First: 2026-11-04T16:26:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T16:26:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generative video models are increasingly used as visual world simulators, yet their primary strength lies in rendering plausible pixels rather than reasoning about how a world should evolve. Coherent world evolution requires more than visual fidelity: when an event occurs, a model must infer its consequences for the entities in the world and then render the resulting state. For example, after a wild beast appears, the model should not only depict the wild beast, but also infer whether a nearby person should flee, take cover, or fight back based on the realized scene. Existing diffusion-based video models lack an explicit mechanism for maintaining world state and reasoning over such event-conditioned consequences, often producing visually plausible but behaviorally static or causally inconsistent outputs. To make this reasoning gap concrete and measurable, we propose a new problem, Out-of-Sight Causal Evolution, which requires a model to render an event, reason about its consequences for the evolving world state, and reveal a causally plausible outcome as the world continues to evolve, even out of sight. As a baseline reference solution, we introduce EvoWorld, a brain-renderer collaboration framework in which a vision-language model serves as the world brain: it observes generated segments, maintains an evolving account of the world, and reasons about plausible state transitions, while a segment-autoregressive video renderer realizes them visually. We further introduce EvoWorld-Data and EvoWorld-Bench for training and evaluating event realization, causal reactions, and revisit consistency. Experiments show that EvoWorld substantially improves event-conditioned causal evolution over recent models while preserving visual quality and consistency, establishing a reference point for reasoning-guided, autonomous world evolution beyond the current field of view.

Generative video models are increasingly used as visual world simulators, yet their primary strength lies in rendering plausible pixels rather than reasoning about how a world should evolve.

Abstract
Training large text-to-image models requires high-quality, curated datasets with diverse content and detailed captions. Yet the cost and complexity of collecting, filtering, deduplicating, and re-captioning such corpora at scale hinder open and reproducible research in the field. We introduce MONET, an open Apache 2.0 dataset of 103.8M image-text pairs collected from 2.9B raw pairs across heterogeneous open sources through successive stages of safety filtering, domain-based filtering, exact and near-duplicate removal, and re-captioning with multiple vision-language models covering short to long-form descriptions, and further augmented with synthetically generated samples. To validate the effectiveness of MONET, we train a 4B-parameter latent diffusion model exclusively on it and reach competitive GenEval and DPG scores, demonstrating that our dataset lowers the barrier to large-scale, reproducible text-to-image research.

Energy-Guided Transport for Projection-Free Physics-Informed Flow Matching
Authors: Giuliano Galadini, Aurelio Uncini, Danilo Comminiello
Venue: NeurIPS 2026

Abstract
Deep generative models, particularly Flow Matching frameworks, have emerged as powerful tools for synthesizing complex physical systems. However, standard architectures remain purely statistically driven and agnostic to the underlying governing equations, often violating fundamental physical constraints during inference. Existing zero-shot approaches designed to enforce these constraints typically rely on rigid algebraic projections. While effective for sparse boundary conditions, applying such discrete geometric corrections to dense, full-domain PDEs may disrupt the probability path, \rev{potentially deviating from the learned manifold and introducing substantial computational overhead}. To address these challenges, we introduce Energy-Guided Transport, a novel training-free framework that formulates non-linear physical constraints as a smooth potential energy landscape. Rather than interrupting the ODE integration, our method applies a continuous dissipative force directly to the intermediate generative states, steering them toward the physical manifold. By evaluating this force implicitly via reverse-mode automatic differentiation, our framework preserves the natural dynamics of the flow while maintaining an efficient $\mathcal{O}(N)$ algorithmic complexity. We extensively evaluate our framework across various 1D and 2D physical systems, including the chaotic Navier-Stokes equations. Our method achieves stable generation without structural divergence, significantly improves the stability-accuracy trade-off compared to state-of-the-art methods, and substantially accelerates inference with respect to exact projection strategies. Code available at: https://github.com/ispamm/Projection-free-energy-guided-flow-matching.

PatchKV: Weight-Space Compensation of KV Cache
Authors: Chanryeol Lee, Chanhyuk Lee, Yeonwoo Choi, Donggyun Kim, Seunghoon Hong
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-09-30T09:01:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T13:53:51+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026. Code available at: https://github.com/cusasak/PatchKV
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Long-context inference with Large Language Models (LLMs) is bottlenecked by the linearly growing memory of the key-value (KV) cache. Existing compression methods reduce the cache through token eviction or approximation, but degrade sharply at aggressive compression budgets. We propose PatchKV, a training-free framework that compensates KV cache compression methods by carrying part of the context in the model's weights. PatchKV pairs an off-the-shelf compressed KV cache with a context-specific weight patch, which is computed once at context-loading time and served for downstream queries for the context. The weight patch is derived in closed form via ridge regression, by aligning the block-wise activations of context-derived reference query tokens under the full cache and the compressed cache. Once merged into the model, the patch leaves the forward graph and per-query inference cost unchanged in the single-context, multi-query setting. Across long-context QA (SCBench with up to 170K tokens, SQuAD, NIAH) and math (GSM8K) benchmarks on three model architectures, PatchKV consistently improves cache compression methods, suggesting an alternative direction to compensate them at aggressive budgets.

Faces in Things, Faces in Nothing: Probing Face Selectivity in Vision Models with Pareidolia
Authors: Qianpu Chen, Derya Soydaner, Rob Saunders
First: 2026-03-04T12:33:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T13:17:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
An electrical outlet can look like a face without depicting a person. Face pareidolia is thus a revealing probe of how vision models turn ambiguous visual evidence into category judgments. When a model calls such an object Human, does that answer depend on its face-like arrangement? Using the FacesInThings dataset, we test six vision models, from a vision-only ViT to CLIP and generative vision-language models, with face detectors as a reference. Each face-like region is paired with three controls from the same photograph that lack the arrangement: a background crop, the region with its tiles shuffled, and its average color. A Human answer driven by the arrangement should appear less often on these controls. We also vary how the categories are described. Three findings emerge. First, models respond to illusory faces, but not as people do: face detectors rarely return a face for these regions, yet CLIP ViT-L/14 and two generative models label roughly half of them Human when the categories are described as kinds of face. Annotators, by contrast, read these faces as non-human, such as animal or cartoon faces. For these models, a face is a human face by default. Second, similar Human rates hide opposite behavior. LLaVA-1.5 answers Human less often once the arrangement is removed; Qwen2-VL calls every average-color patch Human and describes a person in each; CLIP ViT-B/32 calls shuffled regions Human more often than real faces; SmolVLM rarely answers Human, but mostly for face-like regions. Third, rewording the categories changes whether Human answers favor the face at all, removing this preference in LLaVA-1.5 and creating it in CLIP. A Human answer is not evidence of a face: vision models can keep giving it after the face is gone.
Summary / 总结
An electrical outlet can look like a face without depicting a person.
GibbsLens: Training-Free Camera Control via Block-Conditional Gibbs Refinement
Authors: Yuzhu Wang, Xi Ye, Duo Su, Yangyang Xu, Jun Zhu

Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Training-free camera control for pretrained flow-matching video generators is a partial-observation inverse problem: a depth-warped guidance video supplies noisy evidence on a subset of latent sites, which the sampler must reconcile with the pretrained prior. Existing methods struggle to balance the trade-off between trajectory adherence and visual quality and the heuristic guidance-strength tuning lacks robustness. We propose GibbsLens, which resolves this dilemma through a structural change to the sampler: each outer hard-replacement guidance step is augmented with an inner-loop block-conditional pseudo-Gibbs refinement on the unobserved complement at the same noise level, with provable convergence to the partial-observation conditional data law. To accelerate convergence on high-dimensional video latents, we exploit their conditional locality, partitioning the unobserved complement into 3D patches, each tracked by a custom mixing indicator that adaptively freezes converged patches. On RealEstate10K and DAVIS, GibbsLens attains the best FVD against all seven training-free and training-based competitors, outperforming every training-free baseline on every reported metric. Abstract
Active embodied question answering requires agents to acquire evidence through viewpoint changes and physical interaction. The central challenge is to avoid undirected interaction: taking plausible actions without specifying what evidence they should reveal for answering the question. Action plans and accumulated histories alone can leave the informational purpose of an interaction implicit. We introduce EviSeek, a training-free framework that makes question-derived evidence demands explicit acquisition objectives, linking observation-conditioned actions and selectively retained evidence to the information the agent seeks. On 3,436 ESI-Bench questions, EviSeek improves accuracy over the Single-model agent by 7.1 percentage points with Qwen3-VL-32B and 3.7 points with Gemini 3.1 Pro, while improving visual-support-weighted accuracy and reducing mean token usage. These findings support making the informational purpose of interaction explicit, with action selection and evidence retention organized around what the agent needs to learn to answer the question.

DreamRealm: Exploring and Materializing Hallucination Worlds for Trustworthy Vision-Language Models
Authors: Benlei Cui, Chunyang Chai, Yiliang Zhang, Xiaoqian Xia, Chengwen Yao, Song Li, Longtao Huang, Yuwen Zhai, Haiwen Hong
First: 2026-11-04T12:34:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T12:34:12+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) remain prone to visual hallucinations when correct responses depend on subtle visual details. One line of post-training methods constructs visual contrasts through image retrieval, generation, or editing to improve fine-grained perception. However, these contrasts are not built around the model's own hallucinations, making it difficult to ensure that supervision targets its actual perceptual confusions. Another line suppresses hallucinated outputs through response correction or preference learning under fixed image conditions, but leaves the design of supervision signals on the image-input side underexplored. Inspired by how humans correct misconceptions, we introduce DreamRealm, a post-training framework that explores diverse latent hallucinated responses, minimally edits original images to make them valid, and trains the model to assign each response to its supporting image. Our data construction pipeline, RealmForgePipe, mines observed and latent hallucinations from a target model and materializes them through image editing. Using hallucinations mined from Qwen3.5-9B, we construct the Realized Hallucination Dataset (RealmSet). Our training objective, RealmMatch, constructs a likelihood score matrix across image--response combinations and combines supervised learning with bidirectional contrastive losses to reinforce correct matches and suppress mismatches. Compared with the original Qwen3.5-9B model, DreamRealm improves performance across six visual perception and hallucination benchmarks, including gains of 7.26 percentage points in HallusionBench accuracy and 11.34 percentage points in MMVP pair accuracy. Together with ablation studies on data construction and training methods, these results demonstrate that hallucination assignment is a promising paradigm for improving visual perception and mitigating hallucinations.

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate remarkable reasoning capabilities, yet the internal mechanisms driving these multi-step processes remain opaque. Existing mechanistic interpretability approaches often rely on superficial text-pattern co-occurrences or capture only short-term effects, struggling to trace the long-horizon, outcome-driven influence of internal components such as neurons or sparse features in multi-step reasoning.In this paper, we introduce Integrated Policy Gradient (IPG), a novel, training-free framework that brings policy-based attribution into mechanistic interpretability for LLM reasoning. Grounded in \textit{outcome-oriented} and \textit{sequential-influence-aware} principles, it backpropagates outcome-level, non-differentiable behavioral signals (e.g., reasoning outcome correctness) through entire inference trajectories, utilizing path integration to isolate outcome-relevant mediators at component level for reliable attribution. This provides a linear approximation of Causal Mediation Analysis (CMA) in reasoning. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that \method achieves precise localization and reveals mechanistic insights by identifying sparse, concentrated subsets of internal components associated with specific reasoning behaviors. Our method also enables modulation of reasoning capabilities, offering a powerful and reliable framework for understanding LLM reasoning. Abstract
Existing traffic signal control methods typically rely on structured traffic states produced by dedicated perception pipelines. Emerging visual approaches often use idealized simulator images and convert them into intermediate representations before control, limiting their ability to directly exploit roadside visual observations. To bridge both the visual-domain and perception-to-decision gaps, we introduce SynTra-V and Traffic-VL. Abstract
Masked diffusion language models can reduce inference steps by revealing multiple tokens per denoising iteration, but this parallelism is fragile: positions that are individually confident may be unsafe to commit together when their predictions are coupled. Existing training-free samplers such as Top-$k$, Fast-dLLM, and EB-Sampler mainly control how many tokens to reveal, while often ranking candidates by token-wise scores that ignore interactions within the selected set. We propose ADAS, a training-free reranking rule that leaves the base sampler's stopping rule unchanged and greedily discounts each token-wise confidence score according to its attention to already selected positions, weighted by their prediction uncertainty. Entity-Aware Evaluation of OCR Systems for Historical Document Transcription
Authors: Camilo Mariño, Diego Belzarena, Marina Gardella, Ignacio Ramírez, Gregory Randall, Jean-Michel Morel
First: 2026-10-07T12:12:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T10:45:47+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Optical Character Recognition (OCR) is essential for digitizing historical archives, yet standard evaluation metrics such as Character Error Rate (CER) do not necessarily reflect whether meaningful information, such as names, dates, and identifiers, is preserved. In this work, we investigate entitylevel analysis of OCR errors as a complementary perspective for evaluating OCR quality in historical documents. Using a publicly available benchmark of 175 documents from the Berrutti Archive, augmented with 5,209 manually annotated entity mentions that will be released upon acceptance, we compare sequence-recognition OCR systems, OCR-specialized vision-language models, and general-purpose vision-language models. Beyond global transcription accuracy, we analyze entity preservation and recoverability under varying character-level edit tolerances. Our experiments show that OCR rankings depend on the evaluation criterion: while Qwen 3.6 achieves the highest entity coverage across all tested tolerances, several systems with lower exact-match coverage recover substantially when small errors are tolerated, suggesting that many of their errors correspond to localized character-level deviations rather than fundamentally incorrect entity transcriptions. Conversely, some systems remain unable to recover many entities even under relaxed matching. These findings show that OCR quality cannot be fully characterized by aggregate transcription metrics alone and highlight the importance of evaluating how OCR errors affect the preservation and recoverability of meaningful information.

Abstract
Numerical molecular representations underpin virtual screening, property prediction, and materials discovery. Conventional fingerprints are efficient and deterministic but lose structural information through hash-based compression at low dimensionalities, whereas graph neural networks recover expressiveness through compute-intensive, task-specific training. Here, we introduce hyperdimensional fingerprints (HDF), which compose the chemical attributes of atoms and their multi-hop environments through algebraic operations on high-dimensional vectors, producing deterministic representations without any training. Across diverse property-prediction benchmarks, HDF ranks consistently among the best representations across datasets and models. Crucially, HDF encodes structure more efficiently: at low dimensionalities, its distances correlate with graph edit distance more strongly than Morgan fingerprints', yielding effective similarity metrics and competitive nearest-neighbor regression at just 64 components. In Bayesian optimization, HDF-based surrogates are more sample-efficient than Morgan fingerprints with a Tanimoto kernel for most of the tested objectives. These results establish HDF as compact, training-free representations for property prediction, similarity search, and sample-efficient optimization.

Abstract
Monocular 3D object detection offers a low-cost route to 3D perception, but it remains fundamentally ill-posed, since depth must be inferred from a single view. Recent transformer-based detectors learn geometry purely from image features and 3D box annotations, lacking the high-level semantic priors that vision-language models (VLMs) acquire from large-scale pretraining. VLMs offer such priors, but deploying one directly as a detector is impractical due to its heavy inference cost. We propose MonoLaDN, a language-guided denoising framework that transfers the geometric reasoning of a VLM into a monocular 3D detector while keeping the deployed model purely vision-based. During training, a frozen VLM produces structured reasoning about each object's occlusion, depth, dimension, and angle from the image and its ground-truth boxes. We encode this reasoning into queries, perturb them with noise, and train the detector to reconstruct the clean reasoning. Reconstructing the corrupted reasoning forces the detector's depth-guided features to internalize the VLM's geometric priors, so this knowledge remains available even after the reasoning path is discarded at inference, adding no latency or parameters to the deployed model. Experiments on KITTI 3D and nuScenes show that MonoLaDN consistently outperforms state-of-the-art detectors and generalizes well to new domains.

Abstract
Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) becomes increasingly difficult to scale to long multi-view sequences. Anchor-based compression methods such as ZPressor alleviate representation growth by decoding Gaussians only from a compact set of anchor feature maps, but keeping these anchors fixed forces each one to aggregate an increasing number of support views. We identify this representation--fusion mismatch and introduce \textbf{CrossPressor}, a training-free method for scalable anchor-based fusion. CrossPressor maintains reconstruction quality close to dense fusion while achieving up to $26.12\times$ faster cross-view fusion and $1.43\times$ end-to-end acceleration on long input sequences. Abstract

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) have recently been extended to handle multiple input images, but this extension dramatically increases the visual token budget, making visual-token pruning not merely an efficiency option but a practical necessity. Existing visual-token pruning methods, primarily designed for the single-image setting, rely on attention-derived saliency signals to estimate per-token importance. However, this attention-centric design transfers poorly to the multi-image regime: it causes substantial performance degradation on multi-image benchmarks, suffers from strong positional bias, and loses discriminative attention signals as the number of input images increases. To test whether attention-selected tokens are truly important for multi-image reasoning, we conduct two complementary causal probes Leave-One-In and Leave-One-Deletion, which measure token importance by restoring individual tokens to a zeroed visual sequence or removing them from the full sequence. Attention-based pruners provide only marginal gains over random selection, showing that attention is a weak proxy for visual-token importance in multi-image reasoning. Motivated by this gap, we propose Relation and Information Aware Pruning (RI-Prune), a training-free, attention-free pre-decoder selector for multi-image visual token pruning. RI-Prune scores each visual token using a z-normalized combination of three signals computed from projector outputs: query relevance, cross-image correspondence with compact per-image prototypes, and intra image representativeness. By integrating these signals, RI-Prune performs balanced per-image Top-K selection to preserve information across input images. Across multiple multi-image benchmarks and three LVLM backbones, RI-Prune achieves a stronger trade-off between token reduction and task performance than prior pruning methods, particularly under aggressive token budgets.
